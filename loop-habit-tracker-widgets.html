Loop's widgets, and how Kaizenly's differ

Loop actually ships more widget types than Kaizenly does. They're built on a different idea, though — and which idea you want decides the answer.

What Loop gives you

Six widget types, plus a stack widget that cycles through several habits in one slot:

Widget Shows
CheckmarkOne habit, tap to tick today off
HistoryOne habit's calendar of past days
ScoreOne habit's strength over time
FrequencyOne habit, by day of week
StreakOne habit's longest runs
TargetOne habit's progress toward a number

The pattern is in the right-hand column: every Loop widget is about one habit. Six habits you want on the home screen means six widgets, or the stack widget and a swipe to see the rest.

What Kaizenly does with four

Widget Shows Closest Loop widget
Progress plant All of today at once — a plant that grows as you finish habits No equivalent
Single habit One habit: tick it, or start its stopwatch, in one tap Checkmark / Target
Habit heatmap A year of consistency in one grid History
Trend Where progress is heading Score

The two real differences

One widget for the whole day

The Progress plant is the one with no counterpart in Loop. Instead of one habit per widget, it takes today as a whole and turns it into a single thing you read without counting: a seedling in the morning, a full plant by the evening. If your home screen is currently four Loop checkmark widgets in a row, this replaces the row.

A stopwatch on the widget

Loop tracks habits you tick and habits you measure. It has no timer, so it has no timer widget. In Kaizenly, meditation, reading and exercise can run a stopwatch on the habit card, and the Single habit widget can start it without opening the app.

Where Loop is ahead: if you want a per-habit widget for day-of-week frequency or a dedicated streak widget, Loop has those and Kaizenly doesn't. Kaizenly shows the same information, but inside the app on the Statistics and habit detail screens rather than on the home screen.

Which to pick

  • You want each habit charted separately on the home screen — Loop does this well, and it's free and open source. There's no reason to switch.
  • You want today's whole picture in one glance, or a timer you can start from the home screen — that's what Kaizenly's set is built around.
